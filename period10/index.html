📖 科普朗读 · Mountains in Motion

Science often begins with a simple question: how does mountains in motion affect the world around us? The answer is not a single fact but a connected story of matter, energy, living things, and change. Earth's crust is divided into moving plates. Collisions can lift rock. Wind, water, and ice wear mountains down. Fossils reveal past environments. Change continues over long periods. To investigate this topic, students can ask a clear question, make careful observations, and record the results. A useful test should be fair, safe, and easy to repeat. We may compare two conditions, measure a change, or collect evidence over several days. Each detail matters, because one small difference can affect the final explanation. When evidence does not support our first idea, we should not hide it. We should review the method and think again. This way of learning also connects science with daily life. A local park, a family kitchen, or a school garden can all become starting points for inquiry. Students can gather information from books, compare it with direct experience, and communicate their conclusions in simple language. They should also remember that scientific knowledge can improve when new evidence appears. Most importantly, science is a shared process. People ask questions, test ideas, discuss mistakes, and build better explanations together. By reading aloud slowly, marking unfamiliar expressions, and retelling the main idea, learners can improve both scientific thinking and English fluency. Word count: 235 ｜ 建议：先默读，再跟读，最后用英语复述主旨。
