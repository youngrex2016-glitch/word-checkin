📖 科普朗读 · Why Birds Migrate

Science often begins with a simple question: how does why birds migrate affect the world around us? The answer is not a single fact but a connected story of matter, energy, living things, and change. Many birds travel with the seasons. Food and daylight guide timing. Stars and Earth's magnetic field aid direction. Resting places are essential. Migration requires stored energy. To investigate this topic, students can ask a clear question, make careful observations, and record the results. A useful test should be fair, safe, and easy to repeat. We may compare two conditions, measure a change, or collect evidence over several days. Each detail matters, because one small difference can affect the final explanation. When evidence does not support our first idea, we should not hide it. We should review the method and think again. This way of learning also connects science with daily life. A local park, a family kitchen, or a school garden can all become starting points for inquiry. Students can gather information from books, compare it with direct experience, and communicate their conclusions in simple language. They should also remember that scientific knowledge can improve when new evidence appears. Most importantly, science is a shared process.
